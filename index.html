C.H. Robinson: the middleman that moves 37 million shipments a year without owning a single truck.

C.H. Robinson is North America's largest freight broker. Shippers (Walmart, an auto plant, a produce grower) need goods moved. Roughly 450,000 small trucking companies have empty trucks. Robinson sits in between, buys the truck's capacity, sells it to the shipper at a markup, and keeps the spread. This dashboard explains that business from first principles: how the spread is earned, why it whipsaws with the freight cycle, how "Lean AI" changed the cost base, why a $604M Texas jury verdict is now the dominant debate, and what the stock is worth under bull and bear cases.

First principles

Freight brokerage 101: how a middleman makes money moving trucks

Assume you know nothing about logistics. Here is the whole industry in five ideas.

1. The U.S. trucking market is enormous and hopelessly fragmented

About 70% of U.S. freight by weight moves by truck. The supply side is hundreds of thousands of tiny carriers: Robinson's own network has ~450,000 contract carriers, and roughly 72% of its truckload shipments are hauled by carriers with fewer than 100 trucks Bernstein via AlphaSense primer. A shipper with 5,000 loads a week cannot phone 450,000 owner-operators. A one-truck owner-operator cannot find Walmart. Someone has to match them.

Two kinds of freight

  • Truckload (TL): one shipper fills a whole 53-foot trailer. Priced per mile. Robinson's biggest product (~46% of adjusted gross profit) UBS.
  • Less-than-truckload (LTL): several shippers' pallets share a trailer, moved through a hub-and-spoke terminal network owned by carriers like Old Dominion or XPO. Robinson resells LTL capacity and handles the classification paperwork (~20% of AGP).

2. The broker buys capacity wholesale and sells it retail

Robinson never owns the truck. For each load it quotes the shipper a sell rate and then finds a carrier willing to move it for a buy rate. The difference is called net revenue or adjusted gross profit (AGP).

Why "gross revenue" is misleading: the $16.2B headline includes the $13.5B Robinson simply passes through to carriers. Analysts value the company on the $2.7B of AGP and on operating income as a percentage of AGP (Robinson calls this "adjusted operating margin"). When you see "NAST margin 40.9%", that is operating income ÷ AGP, not ÷ gross revenue.

3. Contract vs spot: two ways to price a load

  • Contract (~70% of Robinson's truckload volume): an annual bid where Robinson commits to a fixed sell rate per lane for 12 months UBS, Jefferies. Predictable for the shipper. Dangerous for the broker if carrier costs move.
  • Spot (~30%): priced load-by-load at today's market rate. Reprices instantly. Landstar is a 100% spot model, which is why it repriced faster than Robinson in 2Q26 Jefferies.

The DAT dry van spot rate (a benchmark from load-board operator DAT) is the market's real-time price of a truck. In August 2026 the dry van spot linehaul rate was ~$2.25 to $2.32 per mile ex-fuel and the contract rate ~$2.42 DAT, Aug 2026. In mid-2025 spot was ~$1.80.

4. The spread squeeze: why a freight recovery hurts before it helps

This is the single most important mechanic in the stock.

1
Trucks get scarce (drivers exit, small carriers go bankrupt, regulation removes capacity). Spot buy rates jump. In 2Q26 dry van spot was up 34% year over year Deutsche Bank.
2
Robinson's buy cost rises immediately because it must pay today's rate to find a truck. Its truckload cost per mile rose 29% in 2Q26.
3
But 70% of its sell rates are locked in contracts. Price per mile rose only 25.5%. The gap compressed NAST gross margin by 170bp to 13.1% UBS, Press release.
4
Two to three quarters later contracts reprice at the higher market, buy rates plateau, and the spread widens again. UBS: net revenue per load lags spot rates by 2–3 quarters, so 2Q26's spot surge should show up in 4Q26 and 2027 AGP.

The mirror image is true in a downturn: when rates collapse (2023), contract sell rates stay high for a while and the spread fattens even as volume falls. That is why Robinson's net revenue margin moves inversely to spot rates in the short run.

Why this matters for the numbers you will see

  • Gross revenue tracks spot rates almost one-for-one (UBS: "coincident relationship"). A 23% NAST revenue jump in 2Q26 was mostly price, not volume (+1.5%).
  • AGP is what pays the bills. It grew only 8.6% in NAST despite revenue +23%.
  • Operating income is AGP minus people (~$1.3B) minus SG&A (~$560M). Because Robinson slashed people costs, a given AGP now converts to far more profit than in 2018 or 2022.
  • Volume × AGP per shipment × (1 − cost-to-serve ratio) is the whole model. Section "Drivers" builds this tree with real 2025 numbers.

Glossary quick-hits

3PL = third-party logistics provider (an outsourcer that arranges transport). NAST = North American Surface Transportation, Robinson's trucking segment. Global Forwarding = ocean and air freight, plus customs clearance. Cass Freight Index = a monthly gauge of U.S. shipment volumes from freight-bill payer Cass. Full glossary at the bottom.

Business model

What Robinson actually sells, and to whom

A 121-year-old produce broker that became a $16B logistics platform. Asset-light means ~85% of operating expenses are variable purchased transportation, which is why it has never posted an operating loss through a freight downturn CFRA via primer.

Customers: ~75,000 shippers

Manufacturers, retailers, food companies, auto plants, energy firms. The largest customer is only ~2% of revenue 10-K FY2025. Management is prioritising "key verticals": automotive, retail, healthcare, energy and tech, where cross-border, drop-trailer and seasonal-surge complexity favours a scaled broker Investor Day, Deutsche Bank.

How they buy

  • Annual RFP / contract bids for repeatable lanes (70% of TL volume).
  • Spot quotes for surge or one-off freight, increasingly via API and AI agents that answer in under 32 seconds.
  • Managed transportation (TMC): Robinson runs the shipper's whole transport function as a carrier-agnostic 4PL, then feeds volume into its own brokerage.

Suppliers: ~450,000 contract carriers

Mostly small fleets and owner-operators. Robinson is their sales force: it finds them loads, pays them fast (often with cash advances), and gives them a mobile app (Navisphere Carrier / Driver) to book freight and upload paperwork. For a one-truck operator, Robinson's load board is a primary way to find backhauls Expert call via primer.

The vetting problem

Robinson must check each carrier's authority, insurance and FMCSA safety rating. After Montgomery (May 2026), how well it vets carriers is now a litigable question in state courts, which is the crux of the legal section.

The platform: Navisphere

An internally built, multi-modal transportation management system launched in 2012 and funded by a 2018 pledge to spend $1B over five years on technology StoneX via primer. It is the system of record for shippers (Navisphere Vision: tracking, cost per mile analytics), carriers (Navisphere Carrier) and drivers (Navisphere Driver: geofenced status updates, proof of delivery).

Because the data is proprietary, Robinson trains pricing and routing algorithms on 37 million shipments a year of transaction history, which sub-scale competitors cannot replicate TD Cowen, Jefferies.

Strategy under CEO Dave Bozeman (since June 2023): "One Robinson" + "Lean AI"

Centralise

Robinson historically ran as a federation of branch offices, each with its own customer relationships and pricing. Bozeman collapsed this into one go-to-market, one pricing engine, and centralised carrier procurement (separate from sales, unlike TQL where the same person does both) UBS, Expert calls.

Automate the routine

Over 30 proprietary generative-AI agents now quote, classify, book, track and handle exceptions: 3M+ tasks done without a human. Quote response fell from 17–20 minutes to under 32 seconds; RFQ response coverage rose from 60% to 100% Mizuho, CFRA. Token cost is only ~$1.0–1.5M a year GS HQ visit, Aug 2026.

Higher peaks, shallower troughs

The "North Star" set at the December 2024 Investor Day: grow 2026 adjusted operating income by $220M vs 2024, raised in October 2025 to $336M, giving the $964M–$1.04B 2026 range. The low end is modelled to deliver ~$6.00 EPS even with a −3% market Press release, Deutsche Bank.

NAST

North American Surface Transportation

What: truckload and LTL brokerage across the U.S., Canada and Mexico. ~$11.6B annual gross revenue, more than 50% larger than #2 TQL and ~3x RXO Deutsche Bank. Roughly 14–15% share of a fragmented U.S. truck brokerage market Jefferies.

2Q26: revenue $3.59B (+23.1%), AGP $469M (+8.6%), gross margin 13.1% (−170bp), adjusted operating income $192M, margin 40.9%. Volume +1.5% (TL +0.5%, LTL +2.0%) vs Cass shipments −3.3%: the 13th straight quarter of outgrowing the market.

Inside the quarter: truckload AGP per shipment held flat despite a 34% surge in dry van spot rates (Deutsche Bank called this "highly impressive"); LTL AGP +21.7% on +19.5% AGP per order as shippers pushed freight from TL to LTL to dodge rising truckload costs.

Global Forwarding

Ocean, air and customs

What: Robinson does not own ships or planes. It buys container slots and air cargo capacity in bulk from carriers (Maersk, CMA CGM, airlines) and resells them to importers and exporters, then clears customs. Built via the 2012 Phoenix International deal ($635M) and APC Logistics (Australia, 2016).

2Q26: revenue $897M (+12%), AGP $189M (+0.7%), gross margin 21.1% (−250bp), operating income $63M, margin 33.4% (+470bp y/y). Air AGP +23.4% (AGP per ton +33.5%, tons −7.5%) as armed conflicts tightened air capacity; ocean AGP −2.8% on softer rates UBS, Bernstein.

Why it matters: forwarding is where the Lean AI playbook is only in the "first inning" (vs "third inning" in NAST), so bulls see the most productivity runway here Evercore. It is also the segment exposed to tariffs and transpacific volumes.

All Other & Corporate

Robinson Fresh, Managed Solutions, and what was sold

Robinson Fresh ($47M AGP in 2Q26) is the 1905 legacy: buying, selling and marketing fresh produce for grocers and foodservice. A new GFSI-certified produce logistics centre opened in South Texas in May 2026 for cross-border volumes.

Managed Solutions / TMC ($32M AGP) sells 4PL managed transportation and Navisphere TMS technology. Low capital, sticky, and a funnel that feeds contract and spot freight into NAST.

Europe Surface Transportation (road brokerage in Europe, once headquartered in Poland via the 2012 Apreo deal) was sold to Berlin-based digital forwarder sennder in February 2025, removing a low-margin, sub-scale business and part of the reason 2025 gross revenue fell 8.4%.

Productivity / AI

Lean AI: doing 37 million shipments with 5,500 fewer people

The most important thing that happened to Robinson between 2022 and 2026 was not the freight cycle. It was that the company cut headcount by a third while growing volume faster than the market, by automating the quote-to-cash workflow with in-house AI agents. Whether this is a permanent, structural change or a cyclical cost cut that gets rehired in an upturn is the central fundamental debate.

Headcount vs volume: the decoupling

Year-end employees: 17,399 (2022) → 11,855 (2025) per primer/10-K. Average headcount per Jefferies model: 14,355 (2024), 12,712 (2025), 11,471 (2Q26), 11,038 (2027E). Shipments held at ~37M a year. Shipments per person per day up over 60% since end-2022 in both NAST and GF (management, 2Q26). Management targets single-digit productivity gains "in perpetuity", double digits in 2026 as GF catches up GS HQ visit.

The architecture (why it is cheap)

A three-layer stack: applications on top, a custom "harness" in the middle that injects logistics context and enforces constraints, and interchangeable LLMs and clouds underneath. Big models are only invoked when necessary, which keeps token counts low and grounds outputs in real shipment data Jefferies. Robinson calls the agent ecosystem the "Agentic Supply Chain" and the "Always-on Logistics Planner" Evercore.

Restructuring behind it

2024 program: $45.7M of charges to flatten management and consolidate real estate. 2025 program: three years, $50–75M total, $30.4M taken in 2025, aimed at AI-driven process optimisation and office footprint 10-K.

Structural  The bull view

  • Jefferies: gains are "structural and permanent"; incremental margins on new volume exceed 100%.
  • Both segments hit mid-cycle margin targets at the cycle trough. StoneX: pre-Lean-AI, 2Q26's spot spike would have cost several hundred more basis points of margin.
  • Management to GS: confident it can absorb "significantly more inbound load requests without adding heads" in NAST; GF tools still being rolled out.
  • Mizuho used Robinson as a software-sector case study in real AI adoption.

Cyclical  The bear view

  • Former VPs: margin expansion is "almost entirely" cost takeout; experienced commercial staff left; the company may need to rehire to scale in an upturn Expert calls.
  • Incentive comp is already pushing 2026 personnel to the high end of guidance: leverage partly cyclical Deutsche Bank.
  • Automated quotes are rigid and often pricier than human negotiation; win rate at one customer fell from 90% to 50%.
  • Ex-Uber Freight CCO: legacy brokers' AI prioritises internal cost cuts over customer-facing innovation.

How to referee the debate

Watch three numbers each quarter:

  1. Volume growth vs Cass. If share gains continue while headcount falls, the "relationships walked out" thesis is wrong.
  2. Personnel ÷ AGP. Bulls need this to keep falling toward ~44% (Jefferies 2027E) even as incentive comp rises.
  3. AGP per shipment in NAST TL. Flat in 2Q26 despite a 29% cost surge. If it rises in 4Q26–1H27 as contracts reprice while heads stay flat, incremental margins will show up as guided.

Management's own scorecard: 2026 adjusted operating income of $964M–$1.04B versus $553M in 2023 with a smaller company, on a market that shrank.

The truckload rate cycle

Where we are in the cycle, and what an upturn does to Robinson's EPS

Trucking is a textbook commodity cycle. Rates rise, carriers buy trucks and hire drivers, capacity floods in, rates collapse, carriers go bankrupt, capacity leaves, rates rise again. The 2022–2025 downturn was the longest on record (Bernstein: trucking "ran below cost for three years"). In 2026 the supply side finally broke, which is why spot rates are up 34–36% year over year while shipment volumes are still falling.

Phase 1 (now): supply shock, spread squeeze

Spot buy rates jump faster than contract sell rates. Robinson's NAST gross margin fell to 13.1%. Gross revenue booms (+23%) but AGP grows slowly (+8.6%, all from LTL). Landstar (100% spot) and asset-based carriers with owned trucks capture the upside immediately. Estimates for 3Q26 get trimmed (UBS 3Q26E $1.69 → $1.64).

Phase 2 (4Q26–1H27): contracts reprice

The 2026–27 bid season resets 70% of the book at higher sell rates (Stephens: +10–15% ex-fuel; UBS models +9–10% TL base pricing in 2027). If spot plateaus, the spread widens and AGP per load rises 2–3 quarters after the spot surge. UBS: NAST gross margin back to ~14% in 2027; Jefferies models 2027 AGP +9% and EPS +26% to $7.70.

Phase 3 (2027+): does demand show up?

So far this is a supply-driven upcycle with Cass volumes still negative. GS: "the next lever of spot-rate growth must come from volume demand." If demand recovers, Robinson's fixed-cost base (personnel ÷ AGP at 44%) converts volume into 100%+ incremental margins. If demand stays weak and 2027 Class 8 deliveries add capacity, the cycle rolls over: UBS warns a second year of falling spot rates is "a meaningful headwind".

Competitive moat

Is there a moat in matching trucks to loads?

Historically, freight brokerage had low barriers to entry: a phone, a load board and a $75,000 surety bond. Robinson's argument is that scale, data, technology and now regulation have turned a commodity service into a network business. The bears say a spread business with 15,000 competitors can never be a fortress.

Moat source 1

Scale and network density

$11.6B of NAST gross revenue is >50% larger than #2 TQL ($7.4B in 2025) and ~3x RXO. With 37M shipments a year and 450,000 carriers, Robinson sees more price points on more lanes than anyone. That transaction density lets it (a) find a truck faster in a tight market, (b) price contracts more accurately, and (c) offer carriers denser backhaul opportunities, which attracts more carriers. Deutsche Bank calls this a "self-reinforcing flywheel". Jefferies: the industry is consolidating to a top 5–10.

Moat source 2

Proprietary technology and data

Navisphere is fully owned, so Robinson keeps every byte of shipment, pricing and carrier-behaviour data and trains its own models on it. The AI layer is architected to be cloud- and LLM-agnostic, with a custom harness that injects logistics context and keeps token spend at ~$1–1.5M a year Jefferies, GS HQ visit. Enterprise shippers integrate Navisphere into their own TMS, which creates switching costs Expert call.

Moat source 3 (new)

The regulatory moat after Montgomery

The May 2026 Supreme Court ruling makes brokers suable in state court for negligent carrier selection. Insurance underwriters will now price carrier-vetting rigour into premiums. Management told Goldman three things follow: small brokers cannot get insurance; those that can cannot afford it; shippers move to large, well-insured brokers GS HQ visit, Aug 2026. Jefferies expects the long tail of 15,000+ brokers to shrink. Bernstein agrees on consolidation but says the winners are asset-based carriers, not brokers.

The case against a moat

  • It is still a spread business. Cleveland Research argues price transparency, shorter contracts and industry cost-cutting will push mid-cycle net revenue margins from ~15% toward 10% over time.
  • Relationships walked out the door. Former Robinson VPs say centralisation and an 11–12% headcount cut removed senior account managers; an expert at Middleby reports Robinson's automated quotes are pricier and less flexible, cutting its win rate there from 90% to 50% Expert calls via AlphaSense.
  • Asset-based carriers are integrating. J.B. Hunt, Knight-Swift and Schneider run their own brokerage arms with the advantage of owned capacity and self-insured working layers. Rail intermodal is building direct channels.
  • Disintermediation by AI. TD Cowen flags the long-run risk that shippers use commoditised AI models to run their own matching.
  • Deepest pockets = biggest target. Bernstein: Robinson's scale buys survivability but makes it the preferred defendant in broker-liability suits.

The dominant debate

Montgomery, the $604M Lipe verdict, and the insurance repricing

Until May 2026, a federal law (the FAAAA) shielded brokers from state lawsuits claiming they negligently hired an unsafe carrier. That shield is gone. Ten weeks later a Texas jury handed Robinson the largest broker verdict in history. This section explains what happened, what it could cost, and why analysts disagree by $80 a share about what it means.

A truck operated by Lupus Superior, a small carrier hired by Robinson, rams stopped traffic on I-20 near Jackson, Mississippi. Six vehicles, three deaths. Bull reading  A moat, not a wound

  • Compliance, vetting and insurance towers are fixed costs; sub-scale brokers "whose indemnity is worthless against large verdicts" face terminal pressure (even Bernstein concedes this) Bernstein.
  • Channel checks already show intermediaries shifting volume to large brokers on fears small ones cannot afford insurance Wolfe, Aug 2026.
  • Robinson already vets 450,000 carriers with a scaled compliance team; the marginal cost of doing it better is small relative to $2.7B of AGP.
  • Jefferies: the "headline shock value is doing more work than the underlying facts"; a broker has no control over a carrier's driver, and the vicarious-liability finding is legally novel and appealable.

Bear reading  Asset-light just became liability-heavy

  • Robinson pays <1% of gross revenue on insurance vs ~5%+ for asset-based carriers. Convergence toward "truck-like" pricing is a multi-hundred-million-dollar problem Bernstein.
  • Double hit: small carriers pass their own premium inflation into buy rates, while Robinson competes for contract freight against self-insured asset carriers and cannot fully pass it on. "Hits both the spread and expense."
  • The jury found negligence despite a Satisfactory FMCSA rating, so the standard is now ambiguous. A "catch-22": gathering more driver data to vet better can itself be evidence of control that triggers vicarious liability TD Cowen.
  • State lookback windows of 3–5 years mean reserves for past accidents could hit the P&L before any appeal resolves.
Tariffs & global trade

Global Forwarding: the ocean and air business exposed to trade policy

About a quarter of Robinson's AGP comes from moving containers and air cargo across borders, mostly Asia to North America. Forwarding earns a spread on freight rates, so it is a levered bet on transpacific volumes and on ocean and air rate volatility.

How forwarding makes money

Robinson signs volume commitments with ocean carriers and airlines at wholesale rates, then sells slots to thousands of importers at retail, plus clears customs (a fee business). Gross margin runs 21–25% of gross revenue, higher than trucking, because service content (documentation, consolidation, customs) is larger.

Volatility is the friend and enemy. Ocean AGP swung from +64% to −53% year over year within the 2021–2023 cycle (Jefferies chart). In 2Q26 air AGP rose 23% on +33% AGP per ton as armed conflicts tightened air capacity, while ocean AGP fell 2.8% on softer rates and customs AGP fell 9.4%.

Tariffs and transpacific

  • China tariffs pull import volumes forward and then create air pockets. GS lists "negative impact from tariffs" as an explicit downside risk; ocean and air demand "remaining muted" is the base-case worry.
  • De minimis changes (ending duty-free treatment for small e-commerce parcels) shift volume from air parcel to consolidated ocean and increase customs-brokerage work per shipment, a mixed effect for GF.
  • Sourcing shifts to Vietnam, India, Mexico play to Robinson's footprint (subsidiaries in Vietnam, India, Thailand, Malaysia, Mexico) and to the Laredo cross-border hub.
  • GS tracked GF AGP per day decelerating from +9% in April to +7% in May and +3% in June 2026 as ocean rates and volumes weakened GS 2Q26.

Why bulls still like Global Forwarding

  • Operating margin hit 33.4% of AGP in 2Q26 vs a 30% target, +470–490bp year over year, with headcount down 16.6%. The Lean AI playbook is only in its "first inning" here Evercore, Jefferies.
  • Deutsche Bank: GF margin beat the sell side by 4.8 points; the upside surprise in 2Q was forwarding, not trucking.
  • UBS raised 2027 forwarding GP to $793M (from $717M) after 2Q while cutting NAST.
  • Strategic option: Jefferies lists a potential sale or spin of Global Forwarding as a catalyst.

The debate

Bull vs Bear

The Street is unusually polarised: price targets from $91 (Morgan Stanley) to $237 (Evercore) on a $152 stock. Both camps agree on the facts (2Q26 was a beat, margins hit targets, a $604M verdict is on appeal). They disagree on whether Lean AI is structural and whether Montgomery is a moat or a wound.

Bull case Structural earnings power the market is not paying for

Who: Evercore ($237), JMP ($235), UBS ($230), Susquehanna ($226), Jefferies ($220), Deutsche Bank ($220), Stephens ($215), StoneX ($205), Wolfe ($196).

  1. Cost-to-serve is permanently lower. ~60% productivity gain since 2022, headcount −32%, both segments at mid-cycle margins in a trough. Incremental margins >100% on new volume (Jefferies); 96% flow-through in 2Q26.
  2. The cycle is turning in Robinson's favour with a lag. Dry van spot up 34% y/y in 2Q26 and above contract for the first time since Feb 2022. Net revenue per load lags spot by 2–3 quarters, so 4Q26–2027 AGP should inflect (UBS Street-high 2027 EPS $8.20).
  3. Share gains are real and accelerating. 13 straight quarters of outgrowing Cass; post-Montgomery "flight to quality" pushes shippers to well-insured, scaled brokers; 15,000+ small brokers face an insurance wall.
  4. Balance sheet and buyback. $2B authorisation, ~$220M repurchased per quarter in 1H26, net debt 1.6x EBITDA, $850M–$2B of M&A capacity.
  5. The verdict is a tail risk, not earnings power. Advisory, appealable for 5–7 years, worst case ~$470M above insurance vs ~$840M of 2025 FCF. Jefferies: "headline shock value is doing more work than the underlying facts."
Bull math (Jefferies upside $300): 2027 EPS $8.00 × 37.5x. UBS $230: 2027E $8.20 × 28x. Evercore $237.

Bear case Asset-light just became liability-heavy in a spread business

Who: Morgan Stanley ($91, Underweight), Bernstein ($151–153, Market-Perform), TD Cowen ($152, Hold), CFRA ($165, Hold), Goldman Sachs ($180, Neutral).

  1. Insurance repricing is structural and asymmetric. Robinson pays <1% of revenue for insurance vs ~5%+ at asset carriers. Underwriters are raising excess layers 30–50%, some brokers 3x–9x. Bernstein: $120M hit to 2027 operating income, −$0.78 EPS. Wolfe: −$0.35 per doubling.
  2. The spread gets squeezed from both sides. Small carriers push their premium inflation into buy rates; Robinson competes against self-insured asset carriers on sell rates and cannot pass it all through. Bernstein: a gross margin compression "that does not recover."
  3. Early-cycle margin pain is here now. NAST gross margin 13.1% (−170bp), 3Q26 estimates being cut (UBS 3Q26E $1.69 → $1.64), AGP per day decelerating +9%/+7%/+3% through the quarter (GS).
  4. Cost cuts are partly cyclical and have a commercial cost. Incentive comp is already pushing personnel to the high end; ex-executives report lost relationships and lower win rates; the company may need to rehire in a real upturn.
  5. The multiple should compress. TD Cowen: broker liability warrants a higher risk premium and narrows the historical broker premium over asset-based peers. GS and Wolfe both cut multiples. At 21x P/E the stock is fairly valued, not cheap.
Bear math (Jefferies downside $130): 2027 EPS $7.20 × 18x. Bernstein $151: 2027E $7.05 × 21.5x. TD Cowen $152: $7.25 × 21x.

Reference

Glossary and source list

AGP / net revenue / gross margin
Adjusted gross profit (AGP), also called net revenue, is gross revenue minus purchased transportation and purchased products. It is the broker's real top line. Gross margin or net revenue margin is AGP ÷ gross revenue (13.1% NAST in 2Q26). Adjusted operating margin is operating income ÷ AGP (40.9% NAST), not ÷ gross revenue.
Spot vs contract; DAT; RoadCheck
Spot = priced per load at today's market. Contract = annual bid with fixed lane rates. DAT is the largest load board; its dry van linehaul rate per mile (ex-fuel) is the industry's spot benchmark. RoadCheck is the annual CVSA inspection blitz each May/June that temporarily pulls capacity off the road and spikes spot rates.
TL, LTL, drop trailer, cross-border
Truckload (TL): a full trailer for one shipper. LTL: shared trailer through a carrier's terminal network, priced by freight class (NMFC). Drop trailer: broker/carrier leaves a trailer at the shipper's dock to be loaded later, so drivers do not wait. Cross-border: U.S.–Mexico freight transloaded at Laredo; requires customs and carrier hand-offs.
3PL, 4PL, TMS, Navisphere
3PL arranges transport for a shipper. 4PL (Robinson's TMC) manages the shipper's whole logistics function including other 3PLs. TMS = transportation management system software. Navisphere is Robinson's proprietary TMS and platform, launched 2012.
Cass Freight Index, Class 8, tender rejections
Cass Freight Index: monthly shipments and expenditures compiled by Cass Information Systems from freight bills it pays. Class 8: heavy-duty tractors; orders lead capacity additions by 6–12 months. Tender rejection rate: share of contract loads carriers refuse; rising rejections mean spot is more lucrative than contract, i.e., a tight market.
FAAAA, Montgomery, negligent selection, vicarious liability, joint-and-several
FAAAA (1994) preempts state laws "related to" motor-carrier prices, routes or services; brokers argued this barred state negligence suits. Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II (May 2026) held it does not. Negligent selection: the broker chose an unsafe carrier. Vicarious liability: the broker is treated as the driver's employer. Joint-and-several: any defendant can be made to pay the whole award.
FMCSA, CSA, Satisfactory rating
FMCSA regulates U.S. trucking. Its CSA system scores carriers on safety alerts; a formal rating of Satisfactory is the highest. Lupus Superior held Satisfactory despite alerts, which is why the Lipe verdict alarms the industry.
